TOKEN VS REIT REGULATION IN HONG KONG
--------------------------------------

OPTION A: TOKENIZED STRUCTURE (Our Approach)
- Regulatory Body: SFC (Securities & Futures Commission)
- Required Licenses: Type 1 (Dealing) + Type 9 (Asset Management)
- Investor Eligibility: Professional Investors only (portfolio >HKD $8M)
- Listing: NOT required (private placement)
- Payout Requirement: NO mandatory payout (but 90% payout to attract investors)
- Taxation: Profits tax applies (16.5% on rental income)

OPTION B: AUTHORIZED REIT (Public Listing)
- Regulatory Body: SFC + HKEx (Hong Kong Stock Exchange)
- Authorization: SFC-authorized collective investment scheme under REIT Code
- Investor Eligibility: Public (retail investors can participate)
- Listing: MUST list on HKEx
- Payout Requirement: MUST distribute ≥90% of net income after tax
- Taxation: Tax-transparent (no profits tax if distributes 90%+)

COMPARISON TABLE
----------------
Criteria                Tokenized (Private)     Authorized REIT (Public)
Min Investment          $50,000                 $10,000-20,000 (lot size)
Liquidity               Quarterly redemption    Daily trading (HKEx)
Regulatory Cost         $500k-$1M setup         $3M-$5M setup + $2M/year ongoing
Time to Market          12-15 months            18-24 months (IPO process)
Tax Efficiency          16.5% profits tax       Tax-transparent (if 90% payout)
Investor Base           100-200 PIs             Unlimited retail + institutional
Governance              Simpler (private fund)  Complex (listed company obligations)

OUR RECOMMENDATION: Tokenized Structure for Initial Launch
- Lower cost ($500k vs $3M setup)
- Faster time to market (12 vs 24 months)
- Test demand before going public (can convert to REIT later)

RISK MATRIX (Likelihood × Impact → Mitigation)
----------------------------------------------

CATEGORY 1: TENANT DEFAULT & VACANCY RISK
-----------------------------------------
Risk: Major tenant (e.g., 20% of GLA) goes bankrupt or terminates lease early
Likelihood: Low (15% over 10-year hold) - logistics tenants more stable than office
Impact: Medium (Lost rent 6-12 months until re-leased + leasing commission)
Mitigation Strategies:
1. Tenant Credit Insurance: Purchase credit insurance on top 5 tenants (covers 75% of 12 months' rent loss)
2. Security Deposit: 6 months' rent held in escrow (covers re-leasing period)
3. Lease Expiry Staggering: Ensure <25% of leases expire in any single year
4. Multi-Tenant Design: Divisible floor plates (can split 50,000 sq ft into 4x 12,500 sq ft units)
5. Pre-Leasing: Target 60% pre-leasing before construction completion
KPI Monitoring: Tenant credit ratings (D&B), rent collection rate (target >98%), lease expiry schedule
Quarterly Review: Top 10 tenant financial health check, early renewal discussions 18 months before expiry

CATEGORY 2: RENTAL REVERSION RISK (MARKET RENT DECLINE)
-------------------------------------------------------
Risk: Market rents decline when leases expire, forcing below-contract renewals
Likelihood: Medium (30% probability of 10%+ rent decline in economic downturn)
Impact: Medium (Reduces NOI by 5-10%, affects debt service coverage)
Mitigation Strategies:
1. Long-Weighted Lease Profile: Target 4-5 year average lease term (vs 3-year market)
2. Rent Step-Ups: Build in 3% annual escalations (floor for renewals)
3. High-Quality Assets: Grade A specifications (12m height, 5 ton/sqm) maintain rent premium vs obsolete stock
4. Location Moat: Kwai Chung/Tsing Yi locations have structural supply constraints (limited new land)
5. Flexible Use: Obtain planning permission for both warehousing + light industrial (broadens tenant pool)
KPI Monitoring: Market rent surveys (quarterly by CBRE/Colliers), rental reversion % (target: +5% to +10%)
Annual Review: Independent market study, repositioning strategy if needed (e.g., cold storage conversion)

CATEGORY 3: E-COMMERCE DISRUPTION (PARADOXICAL RISK)
----------------------------------------------------
Risk: E-commerce growth increases logistics demand BUT also threatens traditional retail tenants
Likelihood: High (80% probability of retail tenant churn, but replaced by e-commerce/3PL)
Impact: Neutral to Positive (Net effect: increased demand for last-mile logistics)
Mitigation (Opportunity Positioning):
1. Last-Mile Conversion: Design for high-turnover inventory (wide truck bays, cross-docking capability)
