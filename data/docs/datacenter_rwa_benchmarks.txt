TOKEN CLASSIFICATION FOR DATA CENTER RWA
----------------------------------------
Asset Type: Real Estate Investment + Revenue-Sharing Rights
SFC Classification: "Securities" under Securities & Futures Ordinance (SFO)
Specifically: Collective Investment Scheme (CIS) interests

REQUIRED LICENSES
-----------------
1. Type 1 License: Dealing in Securities (for token distribution)
2. Type 9 License: Asset Management (for managing DC operations on behalf of investors)

NOTE: Unlike solar projects, data centers may also trigger Type 4 (Advising on Securities) if you provide investment advisory services.

SFC LICENSING PROCESS (IDENTICAL TIMELINE TO SOLAR)
---------------------------------------------------
Total Timeline: 15-18 months
Phase 1 (Months 1-6): Corporate setup, compliance manual, hire ROs
Phase 2 (Month 7): Application submission (Form 1, 4, 5 + 24 documents)
Phase 3 (Months 8-15): SFC review, queries, on-site inspection
Phase 4 (Months 16-18): License grant, final setup

CAPITAL REQUIREMENTS
--------------------
Minimum Paid-Up Capital: HKD $10M (same as solar)
Liquid Capital: HKD $3M + 5% of AUM
Professional Indemnity Insurance: HKD $10M minimum

SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS FOR DATA CENTER REIT-LIKE TOKENS
--------------------------------------------------------
1. **Real Estate Valuation Requirements**
   - Annual independent valuation by HKIS (Hong Kong Institute of Surveyors) member
   - Valuation methodology: Income approach (DCF of future NOI) + comparable sales
   - Disclosure: NAV calculation must be disclosed quarterly to investors

2. **Occupancy & Revenue Reporting**
   - Monthly occupancy rate reporting (unlike solar's quarterly energy generation)
   - Customer concentration risk: Disclose if any customer > 15% of revenue
   - Contract maturity profile: Show % of revenue with <1 year, 1-3 years, 3-5 years, 5+ years remaining

3. **Property Management Disclosure**
   - If property manager is related party, must disclose fee structure and rationale
   - Independent facilities audit required annually (similar to solar's energy yield assessment)

4. **Technology Refresh Risk**
   - Data centers face faster obsolescence than solar panels (5-7 years vs 25+ years)
   - Must maintain Capex reserve account (10-15% of annual revenue vs solar's 5%)
   - Disclosure requirement: Expected equipment replacement timeline and costs

LAND BUREAU & BUILDING DEPARTMENT APPROVALS (HK-Specific)
----------------------------------------------------------
1. **Land Lease Modification** (if government land)
   - Industrial land → data center use may require premium payment
   - Lands Department approval: 6-12 months
   - Premium: Typically 1-3x difference in industrial vs special industry land value

2. **Building Plans Approval**
   - Buildings Department submission with structural, MEP, fire services plans
   - Timeline: 4-6 months for approval
   - Key compliance: Fire Services Ordinance (FSO), Building Energy Codes

3. **Power Supply Application**
   - CLP/HK Electric: 12-18 months for 10+ MW capacity
   - Requires dedicated substation if >5MW
   - Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) may be required if >10MW

CROSS-BORDER CONSIDERATIONS (SHENZHEN/QIANHAI ALTERNATIVE)
----------------------------------------------------------
If locating data center in Qianhai (Shenzhen) to serve Hong Kong market:
- **Advantage**: Lower land cost (1/3 of HK), lower power cost (RMB 0.65/kWh vs HKD 1.30/kWh)
- **Regulatory**: Must comply with BOTH CSRC (China) and SFC (Hong Kong) if selling tokens to HK investors
- **Data Sovereignty**: May face restrictions on cross-border data transfer (China Cybersecurity Law)
- **Currency Risk**: Revenue in RMB, debt/tokens may be in USD/HKD

RISK MATRIX (Likelihood × Impact → Mitigation)
----------------------------------------------

CATEGORY 1: TECHNOLOGY & OBSOLESCENCE RISK
------------------------------------------
Risk: Equipment becomes obsolete faster than solar panels (5-7 years vs 25 years)
Likelihood: High (80% probability of major refresh needed by Year 7)
Impact: Medium (reduces competitive pricing power, but does not stop operations)
Mitigation Strategies:
1. Capex Reserve Fund: $1.5M annually (15% of revenue) for equipment upgrades
2. Modular Design: Use hot-swappable UPS and cooling units (minimize downtime during refresh)
3. Technology Escrow: Contractual right to upgrade at cost from equipment suppliers
4. Customer Contract Clauses: Price escalation to pass-through 50% of upgrade costs
KPI Monitoring: PUE trend (if increasing above 1.6, indicates aging efficiency), customer complaints about power/cooling capacity
Quarterly Review: Technology roadmap review with advisory board (include cloud provider representatives)

CATEGORY 2: CUSTOMER CONCENTRATION RISK
---------------------------------------
Risk: Top 3 customers represent 60% of revenue (vs solar's diversified grid sales)
Likelihood: Medium (40% chance of losing 1 major customer in 5-year period)
Impact: High (loss of anchor tenant = 20-30% revenue drop + difficulty re-leasing at same rate)
Mitigation Strategies:
1. Contract Staggering: Ensure no more than 25% of contracts expire in same year
2. Credit Insurance: Purchase trade credit insurance on top 5 customers (covers 80% of loss)
3. Anchor Tenant Incentives: Offer 10-15% discount for 10-year contracts (vs 3-year standard)
4. Diversification Target: No single customer >20% by Year 5 (covenant in debt agreement)
5. Expansion Rights: Give top customers right of first refusal on expansion space (lock-in)
KPI Monitoring: Customer concentration index (HHI), contract renewal rate (target >85%)
Quarterly Review: Credit monitoring of top 10 customers (D&B ratings, financial statement review)

CATEGORY 3: POWER OUTAGE & UPTIME RISK
--------------------------------------
Risk: Power outage causes downtime → SLA breach → service credits + reputational damage
Likelihood: Low (5% annual probability of >10 minute outage in Tier III design with N+1 redundancy)
Impact: Very High (Tier III SLA allows only 1.6 hours/year downtime; breach triggers service credits of 10% monthly fees)
Mitigation Strategies:
1. Redundancy Design: 2N UPS configuration (double backup), N+1 generators (one spare)
2. Fuel Contracts: 72-hour on-site diesel fuel + priority refueling contract with supplier
3. Utility Diversity: Dual grid connections from separate substations (if available in HK)
4. Insurance: Business Interruption covers lost revenue + service credit payouts
5. Testing Protocol: Monthly generator tests, annual full failover test (scheduled maintenance window)
KPI Monitoring: Actual uptime % (target: 99.99%), Mean Time To Repair (MTTR, target <1 hour), number of SLA breaches
Real-time Monitoring: 24/7 NOC with automated alerts, escalation to management if critical systems show anomalies

CATEGORY 4: CYBERSECURITY & DATA BREACH RISK
--------------------------------------------
Risk: Ransomware attack or data breach affecting customer data/operations
Likelihood: Medium (30% probability of attempted attack, 5% probability of successful breach)
Impact: Very High (Reputational damage, regulatory fines under PDPO, customer lawsuits, potential loss of SFC license)
Mitigation Strategies:
1. Cyber Insurance: $10M cyber liability policy (covers breach response, legal, regulatory fines)
2. Segmentation: Customer networks are fully isolated (no cross-customer access)
3. Physical Security: Biometric access (fingerprint + facial recognition), 24/7 security guards, mantrap entries
4. Compliance Certifications: ISO 27001 (information security), SOC 2 Type II (operational controls), PCI-DSS if hosting payment systems
5. Incident Response Plan: Cybersecurity firm on retainer (e.g., CrowdStrike, Mandiant), 4-hour response time SLA
KPI Monitoring: Number of security incidents (target: 0 successful breaches), penetration test results (annual), customer audit requests satisfied (100%)
